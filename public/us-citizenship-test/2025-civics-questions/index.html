128 civics questions and answers (2025 civics test)

This is the complete list of civics questions for the 2025 civics test, which applies to Form N-400 filed on or after October 20, 2025. At the interview the officer asks up to 20 of these questions out loud, and you pass when you answer 12 correctly.

Many questions have more than one acceptable answer. Unless the question asks for more, you only need to give one. Questions marked 65/20 are the 20 questions for applicants who are 65 or older and have been permanent residents for at least 20 years. Answers that change with elections were last updated on November 12, 2026.

American Government

Principles of American Government

  1. 1. What is the form of government of the United States?

    • Republic
    • Constitution-based federal republic
    • Representative democracy

    In a republic, people elect leaders to make laws for them. It is a federal republic because power is shared between the national government and the states.

  2. 2. What is the supreme law of the land? 65/20

    • (U.S.) Constitution

    The Constitution is the highest law in the country, so every federal and state law must follow it. Courts can rule that a law that goes against the Constitution is not valid.

  3. 3. Name one thing the U.S. Constitution does.

    • Forms the government
    • Defines powers of government
    • Defines the parts of government
    • Protects the rights of the people

    Written in 1787, the Constitution creates the three branches of government and lists their powers. Its amendments, including the Bill of Rights, protect people's rights.

  4. 4. The U.S. Constitution starts with the words “We the People.” What does “We the People” mean?

    • Self-government
    • Popular sovereignty
    • Consent of the governed
    • People should govern themselves
    • (Example of) social contract

    These first words show that the government's power comes from the people. Citizens choose their leaders and agree to be governed, which is the idea of self-government.

  5. 5. How are changes made to the U.S. Constitution?

    • Amendments
    • The amendment process

    An amendment is a change or addition to the Constitution. Usually, two-thirds of both the House and the Senate must vote for it, and then three-fourths of the states must approve it.

  6. 6. What does the Bill of Rights protect?

    • (The basic) rights of Americans
    • (The basic) rights of people living in the United States

    The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments, added in 1791. It protects basic freedoms like speech and religion, and the right to a fair trial.

  7. 7. How many amendments does the U.S. Constitution have? 65/20

    • Twenty-seven (27)

    Changing the Constitution is hard, so it has been amended only 27 times since it was written in 1787. The first ten amendments, the Bill of Rights, were added together in 1791.

  8. 8. Why is the Declaration of Independence important?

    • It says America is free from British control.
    • It says all people are created equal.
    • It identifies inherent rights.
    • It identifies individual freedoms.

    Adopted on July 4, 1776, the Declaration told the world the colonies were free from Britain. It also said all people are created equal and have rights like liberty.

  9. 9. What founding document said the American colonies were free from Britain?

    • Declaration of Independence

    The title gives a clue: it declared independence. Adopted on July 4, 1776, it said the 13 colonies were free states, no longer ruled by the British king.

  10. 10. Name two important ideas from the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution.

    • Equality
    • Liberty
    • Social contract
    • Natural rights
    • Limited government
    • Self-government

    Ideas like these shaped both documents: people are born with rights, government gets its power from the people, and government power should be limited.

  11. 11. The words “Life, Liberty, and the pursuit of Happiness” are in what founding document?

    • Declaration of Independence

    These famous words come from the Declaration of Independence, written mainly by Thomas Jefferson. It says all people have these rights and governments exist to protect them.

  12. 12. What is the economic system of the United States? 65/20

    • Capitalism
    • Free market economy

    In a free market economy, people and private companies, not the government, own most businesses. They decide what to make, buy, and sell.

  13. 13. What is the rule of law?

    • Everyone must follow the law.
    • Leaders must obey the law.
    • Government must obey the law.
    • No one is above the law.

    The rule of law means the same laws apply to everyone equally. Even presidents, members of Congress, and judges can be held responsible if they break the law.

  14. 14. Many documents influenced the U.S. Constitution. Name one.

    • Declaration of Independence
    • Articles of Confederation
    • Federalist Papers
    • Anti-Federalist Papers
    • Virginia Declaration of Rights
    • Fundamental Orders of Connecticut
    • Mayflower Compact
    • Iroquois Great Law of Peace

    The writers of the Constitution learned from earlier documents. For example, the Articles of Confederation, the nation's first plan of government, created a weak national government, so the Constitution replaced it.

  15. 15. There are three branches of government. Why?

    • So one part does not become too powerful
    • Checks and balances
    • Separation of powers

    Splitting power among Congress, the president, and the courts lets each branch check the others. This keeps any one person or group from taking too much power.

System of Government

  1. 16. Name the three branches of government.

    • Legislative, executive, and judicial
    • Congress, president, and the courts

    The legislative branch (Congress) makes laws, the executive branch (the president) carries them out, and the judicial branch (the courts) explains them.

  2. 17. The President of the United States is in charge of which branch of government?

    • Executive branch

    The executive branch carries out and enforces the laws. The president leads it, with help from the vice president, the Cabinet, and federal agencies.

  3. 18. What part of the federal government writes laws?

    • (U.S.) Congress
    • (U.S. or national) legislature
    • Legislative branch

    Congress is the legislative branch. A bill must pass both the Senate and the House of Representatives before it goes to the president to sign.

  4. 19. What are the two parts of the U.S. Congress?

    • Senate and House (of Representatives)

    The Senate has 100 members, two from each state. The House has 435 voting members, and states with more people get more of them.

  5. 20. Name one power of the U.S. Congress. 65/20

    • Writes laws
    • Declares war
    • Makes the federal budget

    Article I of the Constitution gives Congress its powers. The president commands the military, but only Congress can declare war, and Congress controls how federal money is spent.

  6. 21. How many U.S. senators are there?

    • One hundred (100)

    Each of the 50 states has two senators, so 50 times 2 equals 100 senators.

  7. 22. How long is a term for a U.S. senator?

    • Six (6) years

    Senators serve six-year terms. Their elections are spread out, so only about one-third of Senate seats are up for election every two years.

  8. 23. Who is one of your state’s U.S. senators now?

    This answer depends on where you live. Choose your state or territory to see its current U.S. senators.

    Every state has two U.S. senators, elected by voters from the whole state for six-year terms. Washington, D.C., and the U.S. territories do not have senators.

  9. 24. How many voting members are in the House of Representatives?

    • Four hundred thirty-five (435)

    After each census, taken every 10 years, the 435 House seats are divided among the states by population. Every state gets at least one representative.

  10. 25. How long is a term for a member of the House of Representatives?

    • Two (2) years

    Representatives serve two-year terms, so every House seat is up for election every two years, in even-numbered years.

  11. 26. Why do U.S. representatives serve shorter terms than U.S. senators?

    • To more closely follow public opinion

    Because they face voters every two years, representatives must pay close attention to what people in their district want. Senators' six-year terms give them more time between elections.

  12. 27. How many senators does each state have?

    • Two (2)

    Every state, large or small, has exactly two senators. This gives each state an equal voice in the Senate.

  13. 28. Why does each state have two senators?

    • Equal representation (for small states)
    • The Great Compromise (Connecticut Compromise)

    At the 1787 Constitutional Convention, the Great Compromise gave states equal representation in the Senate and seats by population in the House. This balanced the interests of small and large states.

  14. 29. Name your U.S. representative.

    This answer depends on where you live. Choose your state or territory to see its current U.S. representative.

    Your U.S. representative is the member of the House elected by voters in your congressional district for a two-year term. You can look up yours by your home address.

  15. 30. What is the name of the Speaker of the House of Representatives now? 65/20

    • Mike Johnson

    This answer can change. It is checked every day.

    The Speaker leads the House of Representatives and is chosen by its members. The Speaker is second in line to become president, after the vice president.

  16. 31. Who does a U.S. senator represent?

    • Citizens of their state
    • People of their state

    A senator represents everyone in their whole state, not just one district. That is why senators are chosen in statewide elections.

  17. 32. Who elects U.S. senators?

    • Citizens from their state

    Since the 17th Amendment in 1913, voters in each state elect their senators directly. Before that, state legislatures chose them.

  18. 33. Who does a member of the House of Representatives represent?

    • Citizens in their (congressional) district
    • Citizens in their district
    • People from their (congressional) district
    • People in their district

    Each congressional district elects one member of the House, who speaks for the people living there. States with more people are divided into more districts.

  19. 34. Who elects members of the House of Representatives?

    • Citizens from their (congressional) district

    Voters who live in a congressional district choose its representative. From the start, the Constitution had House members elected directly by the people.

  20. 35. Some states have more representatives than other states. Why?

    • (Because of) the state’s population
    • (Because) they have more people
    • (Because) some states have more people

    The census counts the population every 10 years. After each count, the 435 House seats are divided again, so a state that grows faster than others can gain seats.

  21. 36. The President of the United States is elected for how many years? 65/20

    • Four (4) years

    Presidential elections are held every four years in November. The new term begins on January 20 of the next year.

  22. 37. The President of the United States can serve only two terms. Why?

    • (Because of) the 22nd Amendment
    • To keep the president from becoming too powerful

    No president served more than two terms until Franklin Roosevelt won four elections. To keep any president from holding power too long, the 22nd Amendment (1951) set a two-term limit.

  23. 38. What is the name of the President of the United States now? 65/20

    • Donald Trump

    This answer can change. It is checked every day.

    The president leads the executive branch and is Commander in Chief of the military. Presidents are elected every four years and can be elected no more than twice.

  24. 39. What is the name of the Vice President of the United States now? 65/20

    • J.D. Vance

    This answer can change. It is checked every day.

    The vice president is elected with the president and becomes president if the president can no longer serve. The vice president also serves as President of the Senate and can break tie votes there.

  25. 40. If the president can no longer serve, who becomes president?

    • The Vice President (of the United States)

    The vice president is first in line to take over. The 25th Amendment says the vice president becomes president if the president dies, resigns, or is removed from office.

  26. 41. Name one power of the president.

    • Signs bills into law
    • Vetoes bills
    • Enforces laws
    • Commander in Chief (of the military)
    • Chief diplomat
    • Appoints federal judges

    Some of the president's powers are checks on the other branches. The president can veto bills passed by Congress and chooses federal judges, who must then be approved by the Senate.

  27. 42. Who is Commander in Chief of the U.S. military?

    • The President (of the United States)

    The Constitution makes the president, an elected civilian, the head of the armed forces. This keeps the military under civilian control, while only Congress can declare war.

  28. 43. Who signs bills to become laws?

    • The President (of the United States)

    After a bill passes both the House and the Senate, it goes to the president. When the president signs it, the bill becomes a law.

  29. 44. Who vetoes bills? 65/20

    • The President (of the United States)

    A veto is when the president rejects a bill passed by Congress. Congress can still make it a law if two-thirds of both the House and the Senate vote to override the veto.

  30. 45. Who appoints federal judges?

    • The President (of the United States)

    The president chooses federal judges, including Supreme Court justices, but the Senate must approve them. This sharing of power is an example of checks and balances.

  31. 46. The executive branch has many parts. Name one.

    • President (of the United States)
    • Cabinet
    • Federal departments and agencies

    The executive branch carries out the laws and includes the president, the Cabinet, and many departments and agencies. USCIS, which handles citizenship, is part of the Department of Homeland Security.

  32. 47. What does the President’s Cabinet do?

    • Advises the President (of the United States)

    The Cabinet is made up of the vice president, the heads of the executive departments, and other top officials. They give the president advice on issues in their areas.

  33. 48. What are two Cabinet-level positions?

    • Attorney General
    • Secretary of Agriculture
    • Secretary of Commerce
    • Secretary of Education
    • Secretary of Energy
    • Secretary of Health and Human Services
    • Secretary of Homeland Security
    • Secretary of Housing and Urban Development
    • Secretary of the Interior
    • Secretary of Labor
    • Secretary of State
    • Secretary of Transportation
    • Secretary of the Treasury
    • Secretary of Veterans Affairs
    • Secretary of War (Defense)
    • Vice-President
    • Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency
    • Administrator of the Small Business Administration
    • Director of the Central Intelligence Agency
    • Director of the Office of Management and Budget
    • Director of National Intelligence
    • United States Trade Representative

    Most Cabinet members lead a federal department and have the title Secretary, like the Secretary of State. The head of the Justice Department is called the Attorney General instead.

  34. 49. Why is the Electoral College important?

    • It decides who is elected president.
    • It provides a compromise between the popular election of the president and congressional selection.

    Voters in each state choose electors, who cast the official votes for president. Each state gets as many electors as it has senators and representatives, and a candidate needs 270 electoral votes to win.

  35. 50. What is one part of the judicial branch?

    • Supreme Court
    • Federal Courts

    The judicial branch is made up of the federal courts. The Supreme Court is at the top, and most federal cases start in lower courts called district courts.

  36. 51. What does the judicial branch do?

    • Reviews laws
    • Explains laws
    • Resolves disputes (disagreements) about the law
    • Decides if a law goes against the (U.S.) Constitution

    Courts decide what laws mean and settle disagreements about them. The power to decide that a law goes against the Constitution is called judicial review.

  37. 52. What is the highest court in the United States? 65/20

    • Supreme Court

    The Supreme Court has the final say on cases about federal law and the Constitution. All lower courts must follow its decisions.

  38. 53. How many seats are on the Supreme Court?

    • Nine (9)

    The Supreme Court has one Chief Justice and eight Associate Justices. Congress sets the number by law, and it has been nine since 1869.

  39. 54. How many Supreme Court justices are usually needed to decide a case?

    • Five (5)

    Five is a majority of the nine justices. When most of the justices agree on how to decide a case, their view becomes the Court's ruling.

  40. 55. How long do Supreme Court justices serve?

    • (For) life
    • Lifetime appointment
    • (Until) retirement

    Supreme Court justices have no term limit. They usually serve until they retire or die, although Congress has the power to impeach and remove a justice.

  41. 56. Supreme Court justices serve for life. Why?

    • To be independent (of politics)
    • To limit outside (political) influence

    Justices never face reelection, so they do not need to worry about keeping their jobs. This lets them decide cases based on the law instead of political pressure.

  42. 57. Who is the Chief Justice of the United States now?

    • John G. Roberts, Jr.

    This answer can change. It is checked every day.

    The Chief Justice heads the Supreme Court and is one of its nine justices. Like all justices, the Chief Justice is appointed by the president, approved by the Senate, and can serve for life.

  43. 58. Name one power that is only for the federal government.

    • Print paper money
    • Mint coins
    • Declare war
    • Create an army
    • Make treaties
    • Set foreign policy

    These powers belong to the national government so the country acts as one nation, with one kind of money and one voice with other countries. States cannot print money or make treaties.

  44. 59. Name one power that is only for the states.

    • Provide schooling and education
    • Provide protection (police)
    • Provide safety (fire departments)
    • Give a driver’s license
    • Approve zoning and land use

    Sharing power between the national government and the states is called federalism. It is why rules for schools and driver's licenses can differ from state to state.

  45. 60. What is the purpose of the 10th Amendment?

    • (It states that the) powers not given to the federal government belong to the states or to the people.

    The 10th Amendment, the last part of the Bill of Rights, was added in 1791. It reflects the idea that the national government has only the powers the Constitution gives it.

  46. 61. Who is the governor of your state now? 65/20

    This answer depends on where you live. Choose your state or territory to see its current governor.

    The governor leads a state's executive branch, a bit like a president for the state, and is elected by the state's voters. Washington, D.C., has a mayor instead of a governor.

  47. 62. What is the capital of your state?

    This answer depends on where you live. Choose your state or territory to see its current capital.

    A state capital is the city where the state government is based, including the governor's office and the state legislature. Often, it is not the state's largest city.

Rights and Responsibilities

  1. 63. There are four amendments to the U.S. Constitution about who can vote. Describe one of them.

    • Citizens eighteen (18) and older (can vote).
    • You don’t have to pay (a poll tax) to vote.
    • Any citizen can vote. (Women and men can vote.)
    • A male citizen of any race (can vote).

    The 15th Amendment protects voting for citizens of any race, the 19th for women, and the 26th for citizens 18 and older. The 24th bans poll taxes (fees to vote) in federal elections.

  2. 64. Who can vote in federal elections, run for federal office, and serve on a jury in the United States?

    • Citizens
    • Citizens of the United States
    • U.S. citizens

    Permanent residents (green card holders) share many freedoms with citizens. But voting in federal elections, running for federal office, and serving on a jury are for citizens only.

  3. 65. What are three rights of everyone living in the United States?

    • Freedom of expression
    • Freedom of speech
    • Freedom of assembly
    • Freedom to petition the government
    • Freedom of religion
    • The right to bear arms

    These rights come from the First and Second Amendments in the Bill of Rights. They protect everyone living in the United States, not only citizens.

  4. 66. What do we show loyalty to when we say the Pledge of Allegiance? 65/20

    • The United States
    • The flag

    The Pledge begins, 'I pledge allegiance to the Flag of the United States of America, and to the Republic for which it stands.' Allegiance means loyalty.

  5. 67. Name two promises that new citizens make in the Oath of Allegiance.

    • Give up loyalty to other countries
    • Defend the (U.S.) Constitution
    • Obey the laws of the United States
    • Serve in the military (if needed)
    • Serve (help, do important work for) the nation (if needed)
    • Be loyal to the United States

    New citizens say the Oath of Allegiance out loud at a naturalization ceremony. Taking the oath is the final step to becoming a U.S. citizen.

  6. 68. How can people become United States citizens?

    • Be born in the United States, under the conditions set by the 14th Amendment
    • Naturalize
    • Derive citizenship (under conditions set by Congress)

    Naturalization is the process immigrants use to become citizens, and this civics test is part of it. Some people are citizens by birth, and some children get citizenship through their parents.

  7. 69. What are two examples of civic participation in the United States?

    • Vote
    • Run for office
    • Join a political party
    • Help with a campaign
    • Join a civic group
    • Join a community group
    • Give an elected official your opinion (on an issue)
    • Contact elected officials
    • Support or oppose an issue or policy
    • Write to a newspaper

    Civic participation means taking part in your community and in government. It helps elected leaders know what the people they represent want.

  8. 70. What is one way Americans can serve their country?

    • Vote
    • Pay taxes
    • Obey the law
    • Serve in the military
    • Run for office
    • Work for local, state, or federal government

    Serving your country is not only about the military. Everyday actions like voting, paying taxes, and obeying the law help keep the government and communities working.

  9. 71. Why is it important to pay federal taxes?

    • Required by law
    • All people pay to fund the federal government
    • Required by the (U.S.) Constitution (16th Amendment)
    • Civic duty

    Federal taxes pay for things the national government does, such as the military, highways, and federal courts. The 16th Amendment, added in 1913, lets Congress collect an income tax.

  10. 72. It is important for all men age 18 through 25 to register for the Selective Service. Name one reason why.

    • Required by law
    • Civic duty
    • Makes the draft fair, if needed

    Selective Service keeps a list of men who could be called to serve if a military draft is ever needed. Most immigrant men ages 18 through 25 living in the U.S. must register too.

American History

Colonial Period and Independence

  1. 73. The colonists came to America for many reasons. Name one.

    • Freedom
    • Political liberty
    • Religious freedom
    • Economic opportunity
    • Escape persecution

    Many early colonists, like the Pilgrims who came on the Mayflower in 1620, wanted religious freedom. Others came for economic opportunity, such as the chance to own land.

  2. 74. Who lived in America before the Europeans arrived? 65/20

    • American Indians
    • Native Americans

    Native Americans, also called American Indians, lived across North America for thousands of years before Europeans arrived. They formed many nations with different languages and cultures.

  3. 75. What group of people was taken and sold as slaves?

    • Africans
    • People from Africa

    Millions of Africans were captured, taken by ship across the Atlantic Ocean, and sold into slavery in the Americas. Slavery in the United States ended in 1865 with the 13th Amendment.

  4. 76. What war did the Americans fight to win independence from Britain?

    • American Revolution
    • The (American) Revolutionary War
    • War for (American) Independence

    The American Revolution was fought from 1775 to 1783. The Americans won with help from France, and Britain recognized American independence in the Treaty of Paris in 1783.

  5. 77. Name one reason why the Americans declared independence from Britain.

    • High taxes
    • Taxation without representation
    • British soldiers stayed in Americans’ houses (boarding, quartering)
    • They did not have self-government
    • Boston Massacre
    • Boston Tea Party (Tea Act)
    • Stamp Act
    • Sugar Act
    • Townshend Acts
    • Intolerable (Coercive) Acts

    Britain passed new taxes on the colonies, like the Stamp Act of 1765, but colonists had no representatives in the British Parliament. A popular protest slogan was 'No taxation without representation.'

  6. 78. Who wrote the Declaration of Independence? 65/20

    • (Thomas) Jefferson

    Thomas Jefferson of Virginia wrote the first draft in 1776, and the Continental Congress adopted it on July 4. Jefferson later became the third president.

  7. 79. When was the Declaration of Independence adopted?

    • July 4, 1776

    The Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776, in Philadelphia. That is why Americans celebrate Independence Day every July 4.

  8. 80. The American Revolution had many important events. Name one.

    • (Battle of) Bunker Hill
    • Declaration of Independence
    • Washington Crossing the Delaware (Battle of Trenton)
    • (Battle of) Saratoga
    • Valley Forge (Encampment)
    • (Battle of) Yorktown (British surrender at Yorktown)

    Bunker Hill (1775) was an early battle. The win at Saratoga (1777) helped bring France in as an ally, and the British surrender at Yorktown (1781) ended the major fighting.

  9. 81. There were 13 original states. Name five.

    • New Hampshire
    • Massachusetts
    • Rhode Island
    • Connecticut
    • New York
    • New Jersey
    • Pennsylvania
    • Delaware
    • Maryland
    • Virginia
    • North Carolina
    • South Carolina
    • Georgia

    The 13 original states began as British colonies along the Atlantic coast, from New England south to Georgia. The 13 stripes on the flag stand for them.

  10. 82. What founding document was written in 1787?

    • (U.S.) Constitution

    Delegates met in Philadelphia from May to September 1787 and signed the Constitution on September 17. That date is now Constitution Day and Citizenship Day.

  11. 83. The Federalist Papers supported the passage of the U.S. Constitution. Name one of the writers.

    • (James) Madison
    • (Alexander) Hamilton
    • (John) Jay
    • Publius

    Madison, Hamilton, and Jay wrote 85 essays in 1787 and 1788 to convince New Yorkers to support the new Constitution. They signed them with one shared pen name, 'Publius.'

  12. 84. Why were the Federalist Papers important?

    • They helped people understand the (U.S.) Constitution.
    • They supported passing the (U.S.) Constitution.

    The essays explained how the new government would work and argued that it would protect people's liberty. Today, judges and students still read them to understand the ideas behind the Constitution.

  13. 85. Benjamin Franklin is famous for many things. Name one.

    • Founded the first free public libraries
    • First Postmaster General of the United States
    • Helped write the Declaration of Independence
    • Inventor
    • U.S. diplomat

    Franklin's inventions include the lightning rod and the Franklin stove. As a U.S. diplomat in France, he helped win French support during the Revolutionary War.

  14. 86. George Washington is famous for many things. Name one. 65/20

    • “Father of Our Country”
    • First president of the United States
    • General of the Continental Army
    • President of the Constitutional Convention

    Washington led the army that won independence, then became the first president in 1789. Because of his role in creating the nation, he is called the 'Father of Our Country.'

  15. 87. Thomas Jefferson is famous for many things. Name one.

    • Writer of the Declaration of Independence
    • Third president of the United States
    • Doubled the size of the United States (Louisiana Purchase)
    • First Secretary of State
    • Founded the University of Virginia
    • Writer of the Virginia Statute on Religious Freedom

    Jefferson's face is on the nickel and the $2 bill. As the third president, he made the Louisiana Purchase from France in 1803, which doubled the size of the country.

  16. 88. James Madison is famous for many things. Name one.

    • “Father of the Constitution”
    • Fourth president of the United States
    • President during the War of 1812
    • One of the writers of the Federalist Papers

    Madison is called the 'Father of the Constitution' because his ideas shaped its plan of government. Later, as the fourth president, he led the country during the War of 1812.

  17. 89. Alexander Hamilton is famous for many things. Name one.

    • First Secretary of the Treasury
    • One of the writers of the Federalist Papers
    • Helped establish the First Bank of the United States
    • Aide to General George Washington
    • Member of the Continental Congress

    Hamilton was an immigrant, born in the Caribbean, who became Washington's aide and the first Secretary of the Treasury. He helped set up the nation's financial system, including a national bank.

1800s

  1. 90. What territory did the United States buy from France in 1803?

    • Louisiana Territory
    • Louisiana

    In 1803, under President Jefferson, the U.S. paid France about $15 million for the Louisiana Territory, west of the Mississippi River. It doubled the size of the country.

  2. 91. Name one war fought by the United States in the 1800s.

    • War of 1812
    • Mexican-American War
    • Civil War
    • Spanish-American War

    The War of 1812 was against Britain, the Mexican-American War (1846-1848) against Mexico, and the Spanish-American War (1898) against Spain. The Civil War (1861-1865) was between the North and the South.

  3. 92. Name the U.S. war between the North and the South.

    • The Civil War

    Eleven Southern states tried to leave the United States and formed the Confederacy. The Civil War lasted from 1861 to 1865, and the North (the Union) won, keeping the country together.

  4. 93. The Civil War had many important events. Name one.

    • (Battle of) Fort Sumter
    • Emancipation Proclamation
    • (Battle of) Vicksburg
    • (Battle of) Gettysburg
    • Sherman’s March
    • (Surrender at) Appomattox
    • (Battle of) Antietam/Sharpsburg
    • Lincoln was assassinated.

    The war began when Confederate forces fired on Fort Sumter in 1861. Gettysburg (1863) was one of its largest battles, and the war ended soon after General Lee surrendered at Appomattox in 1865.

  5. 94. Abraham Lincoln is famous for many things. Name one. 65/20

    • Freed the slaves (Emancipation Proclamation)
    • Saved (or preserved) the Union
    • Led the United States during the Civil War
    • 16th president of the United States
    • Delivered the Gettysburg Address

    Lincoln was president during the Civil War and worked to keep the country united. In his 1863 Gettysburg Address, he described government 'of the people, by the people, for the people.'

  6. 95. What did the Emancipation Proclamation do?

    • Freed the slaves
    • Freed slaves in the Confederacy
    • Freed slaves in the Confederate states
    • Freed slaves in most Southern states

    President Lincoln issued it on January 1, 1863, during the Civil War, to free enslaved people in the Confederate states. The 13th Amendment ended slavery in the whole country in 1865.

  7. 96. What U.S. war ended slavery?

    • The Civil War

    The North's victory in the Civil War (1861-1865) led to the end of slavery. The 13th Amendment, added in December 1865, officially ended slavery throughout the country.

  8. 97. What amendment says all persons born or naturalized in the United States, and subject to the jurisdiction thereof, are U.S. citizens?

    • 14th Amendment

    Added in 1868, after the Civil War, the 14th Amendment made formerly enslaved people citizens. It also says states must give all people equal protection of the laws.

  9. 98. When did all men get the right to vote?

    • After the Civil War
    • During Reconstruction
    • (With the) 15th Amendment
    • 1870

    The 15th Amendment was added in 1870, during Reconstruction, the period after the Civil War. It says citizens cannot be denied the vote because of race or color, or because they were once enslaved.

  10. 99. Name one leader of the women’s rights movement in the 1800s.

    • Susan B. Anthony
    • Elizabeth Cady Stanton
    • Sojourner Truth
    • Harriet Tubman
    • Lucretia Mott
    • Lucy Stone

    In 1848, Elizabeth Cady Stanton and Lucretia Mott helped organize the first U.S. women's rights convention, in Seneca Falls, New York. Susan B. Anthony later worked for decades for women's right to vote.

Recent American History and Other Important Historical Information

  1. 100. Name one war fought by the United States in the 1900s.

    • World War I
    • World War II
    • Korean War
    • Vietnam War
    • (Persian) Gulf War

    The U.S. fought in both world wars, then in Korea and Vietnam during the Cold War. The Persian Gulf War, in 1991, forced Iraq's army out of Kuwait.

  2. 101. Why did the United States enter World War I?

    • Because Germany attacked U.S. (civilian) ships
    • To support the Allied Powers (England, France, Italy, and Russia)
    • To oppose the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria)

    After German submarines sank American ships, President Woodrow Wilson asked Congress to declare war on Germany in April 1917. The U.S. then fought on the side of the Allied Powers.

  3. 102. When did all women get the right to vote?

    • 1920
    • After World War I
    • (With the) 19th Amendment

    The 19th Amendment, added in 1920, says the right to vote cannot be denied because of sex. It came after more than 70 years of work by women's rights leaders.

  4. 103. What was the Great Depression?

    • Longest economic recession in modern history

    During the Great Depression, which lasted through the 1930s, many banks and businesses failed. By 1933, about one in four American workers had no job.

  5. 104. When did the Great Depression start?

    • The Great Crash (1929)
    • Stock market crash of 1929

    Stock prices crashed in October 1929, and many investors lost large amounts of money. In the years that followed, thousands of banks failed and millions of people lost their jobs.

  6. 105. Who was president during the Great Depression and World War II?

    • (Franklin) Roosevelt

    Franklin D. Roosevelt (FDR) was elected four times, more than any other president, and served from 1933 until his death in 1945. His New Deal programs were created to fight the Depression.

  7. 106. Why did the United States enter World War II?

    • (Bombing of) Pearl Harbor
    • Japanese attacked Pearl Harbor
    • To support the Allied Powers (England, France, and Russia)
    • To oppose the Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, and Japan)

    Japan attacked the U.S. naval base at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii, on December 7, 1941. The next day the U.S. declared war on Japan, and Germany and Italy soon declared war on the U.S.

  8. 107. Dwight Eisenhower is famous for many things. Name one.

    • General during World War II
    • President at the end of (during) the Korean War
    • 34th president of the United States
    • Signed the Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956 (Created the Interstate System)

    As a general in World War II, Eisenhower led the D-Day invasion of France in 1944. As president from 1953 to 1961, he signed the law that created the Interstate Highway System.

  9. 108. Who was the United States’ main rival during the Cold War?

    • Soviet Union
    • USSR
    • Russia

    The Soviet Union (USSR) was a large communist country, and Russia was its biggest part. The Cold War began after World War II and ended when the Soviet Union broke apart in 1991.

  10. 109. During the Cold War, what was one main concern of the United States?

    • Communism
    • Nuclear war

    The Cold War was called 'cold' because the U.S. and the Soviet Union never fought each other directly. Both built nuclear weapons, and the U.S. worked to stop communism from spreading.

  11. 110. Why did the United States enter the Korean War?

    • To stop the spread of communism

    In 1950, communist North Korea invaded South Korea. The U.S. led United Nations forces to defend the South, and the fighting ended with a cease-fire agreement in 1953.

  12. 111. Why did the United States enter the Vietnam War?

    • To stop the spread of communism

    The U.S. supported South Vietnam against communist North Vietnam. Large numbers of U.S. troops fought there from 1965 to 1973, and the war ended in 1975.

  13. 112. What did the civil rights movement do?

    • Fought to end racial discrimination

    In the 1950s and 1960s, people used peaceful protests to fight segregation (separating people by race). Their work helped pass the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

  14. 113. Martin Luther King, Jr. is famous for many things. Name one. 65/20

    • Fought for civil rights
    • Worked for equality for all Americans
    • Worked to ensure that people would “not be judged by the color of their skin, but by the content of their character”

    King, a minister, led peaceful protests against racial discrimination. In his 1963 'I Have a Dream' speech, he shared his dream that people would be judged by their character, not their skin color.

  15. 114. Why did the United States enter the Persian Gulf War?

    • To force the Iraqi military from Kuwait

    In August 1990, Iraq invaded its small neighbor Kuwait. In early 1991, the U.S. led a group of many countries that drove the Iraqi army out of Kuwait.

  16. 115. What major event happened on September 11, 2001 in the United States? 65/20

    • Terrorists attacked the United States
    • Terrorists took over two planes and crashed them into the World Trade Center in New York City
    • Terrorists took over a plane and crashed into the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia
    • Terrorists took over a plane originally aimed at Washington, D.C., and crashed in a field in Pennsylvania

    On September 11, 2001, terrorists took over four airplanes. Nearly 3,000 people were killed, making it the deadliest terrorist attack in U.S. history.

  17. 116. Name one U.S. military conflict after the September 11, 2001 attacks.

    • (Global) War on Terror
    • War in Afghanistan
    • War in Iraq

    The U.S. went to war in Afghanistan in October 2001 against al-Qaeda, the group behind the attacks, and the Taliban, who protected it. The Iraq War began in 2003.

  18. 117. Name one American Indian tribe in the United States.

    • Apache
    • Blackfeet
    • Cayuga
    • Cherokee
    • Cheyenne
    • Chippewa
    • Choctaw
    • Creek
    • Crow
    • Hopi
    • Huron
    • Inupiat
    • Lakota
    • Mohawk
    • Mohegan
    • Navajo
    • Oneida
    • Onondaga
    • Pueblo
    • Seminole
    • Seneca
    • Shawnee
    • Sioux
    • Teton
    • Tuscarora

    There are hundreds of American Indian tribes in the United States, each with its own history, culture, and government. The Cherokee and the Navajo are two of the largest.

  19. 118. Name one example of an American innovation.

    • Light bulb
    • Automobile (cars, internal combustion engine)
    • Skyscrapers
    • Airplane
    • Assembly line
    • Landing on the moon
    • Integrated circuit (IC)

    The Wright brothers made the first successful powered airplane flight in 1903, and Henry Ford's assembly line made cars cheaper. In 1969, American astronauts became the first people to walk on the moon.

Symbols and Holidays

Symbols

  1. 119. What is the capital of the United States?

    • Washington, D.C.

    D.C. stands for District of Columbia, a federal district that is not part of any state. The city is named after George Washington and has been the capital since 1800.

  2. 120. Where is the Statue of Liberty?

    • New York (Harbor)
    • Liberty Island
    • New Jersey
    • near New York City
    • on the Hudson (River)

    The Statue of Liberty was a gift from the people of France, dedicated in 1886. It stands on Liberty Island in New York Harbor and became a symbol of welcome for immigrants.

  3. 121. Why does the flag have 13 stripes? 65/20

    • (Because there were) 13 original colonies
    • (Because the stripes) represent the original colonies

    The 13 red and white stripes stand for the 13 colonies that declared independence in 1776. The stripes stay at 13, but a star is added for each new state.

  4. 122. Why does the flag have 50 stars?

    • (Because there is) one star for each state
    • (Because) each star represents a state
    • (Because there are) 50 states

    Each of the 50 stars stands for one state. The 50-star flag has been used since 1960, after Hawaii became the 50th state in 1959.

  5. 123. What is the name of the national anthem?

    • The Star-Spangled Banner

    Francis Scott Key wrote the words during the War of 1812, when he saw the flag still flying over Fort McHenry after a British attack in 1814. It became the national anthem in 1931.

  6. 124. The Nation’s first motto was “E Pluribus Unum.” What does that mean?

    • Out of many, one
    • We all become one

    This Latin motto describes the 13 colonies uniting as one nation, and it is on the Great Seal and U.S. coins. 'In God We Trust' became the official national motto in 1956.

Holidays

  1. 125. What is Independence Day?

    • A holiday to celebrate U.S. independence (from Britain)
    • The country’s birthday

    Independence Day is July 4, the day in 1776 when the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence. Americans often celebrate with parades, picnics, and fireworks.

  2. 126. Name three national U.S. holidays. 65/20

    • New Year’s Day
    • Martin Luther King, Jr. Day
    • Presidents Day (Washington’s Birthday)
    • Memorial Day
    • Juneteenth
    • Independence Day
    • Labor Day
    • Columbus Day
    • Veterans Day
    • Thanksgiving Day
    • Christmas Day

    These are federal holidays, when most federal offices close. Juneteenth, on June 19, became a federal holiday in 2021 and celebrates the end of slavery in the United States.

  3. 127. What is Memorial Day?

    • A holiday to honor soldiers who died in military service

    Memorial Day is the last Monday in May. It began after the Civil War as Decoration Day, when people put flowers on the graves of soldiers who died.

  4. 128. What is Veterans Day?

    • A holiday to honor people in the (U.S.) military
    • A holiday to honor people who have served (in the U.S. military)

    Veterans Day is November 11, the day World War I fighting ended in 1918. Unlike Memorial Day, which honors those who died, it honors everyone who has served in the military.
