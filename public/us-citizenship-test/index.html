U.S. citizenship test: civics questions, answers and free practice

At your naturalization interview, a USCIS officer asks you civics questions about American government and history. You can prepare for every one of them: the full list of questions and answers is public. This guide has both versions of the test, the answers that are correct today, and a free practice interview.

How to study

  1. Confirm your version. Check the date you filed Form N-400, then use the 2025 or the 2008 list.
  2. Read the list once from start to finish. Each question page explains why the answer is what it is, which makes it far easier to remember.
  3. Pick one answer per question. Most questions accept several answers. Choose the one that is shortest and easiest for you to say.
  4. Learn your own answers. Write down your two senators, your representative, your governor and your state capital.
  5. Practice out loud every day. A few minutes a day works better than one long session. Repeat the questions you miss until you stop missing them.

Common questions

Which version of the civics test will I take?

It depends on when you filed Form N-400. If you filed on or after October 20, 2025, you take the 2025 civics test (128 questions). If you filed before that date, you take the 2008 civics test (100 questions).

How many questions are on the U.S. citizenship test?

On the 2025 test, the officer asks up to 20 questions from the list of 128, and you must answer 12 correctly. On the 2008 test, the officer asks up to 10 questions from the list of 100, and you must answer 6 correctly.

Is the civics test multiple choice?

No. It is an oral test. A USCIS officer asks each question out loud in English and you answer out loud. That is why it helps to practice by speaking your answers, not only reading them.

Does the officer ask every question?

No. The officer stops as soon as the result is certain. On the 2025 test that means the test ends when you have 12 correct answers, or when you have 9 incorrect answers.

What is the 65/20 rule?

If you are 65 or older and have been a lawful permanent resident for 20 years or more, you study only 20 specially marked questions. The officer asks 10 of them and you need 6 correct, and you may take the test in the language of your choice. See the 65/20 questions.

Which answers change over time?

The names of the President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, Chief Justice, and your own senators, representative and governor. You must give the answer that is correct on the day of your interview. Oathly checks these every day, and USCIS lists them on its test updates page.

What happens if I do not pass?

USCIS gives you one more chance to take the part you did not pass, usually between 60 and 90 days after your first interview. Check your own notice and the official USCIS study page for the rules that apply to you.

Is there also an English test?

Yes. Besides civics, the officer checks your English: speaking (during the interview itself), reading (you read one of up to three sentences aloud correctly) and writing (you write one of up to three sentences correctly). Some applicants are exempt from the English requirement because of age and time as a permanent resident.
