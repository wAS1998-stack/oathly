Part 1: Australia and its people

51 practice questions with answers and explanations for Part 1 of the Australian citizenship test. The correct answer is marked under each question.

Take a practice test

  1. 1. Which peoples are Australia's first inhabitants?

    • Convicts who arrived from Great Britain
    • Free settlers from Great Britain and Ireland
    • Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples (correct)

    Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples were the first people to live in Australia, long before European settlement.

  2. 2. Which statement about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples is correct?

    • They have the oldest continuous cultures and traditions in the world (correct)
    • They arrived in Australia with the First Fleet
    • They came to Australia during the gold rush

    Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultures and traditions are the oldest continuous ones in the world.

  3. 3. According to the archaeological record, when did Aboriginal peoples arrive in Australia?

    • Between 6,500 and 4,000 years ago
    • About 1,000 years ago
    • Between 65,000 and 40,000 years ago (correct)

    Archaeological evidence shows that Aboriginal peoples arrived in Australia between 65,000 and 40,000 years ago.

  4. 4. Where are Torres Strait Islander people from?

    • Islands in Bass Strait, between Victoria and Tasmania
    • Islands between the northern tip of Queensland and Papua New Guinea (correct)
    • Islands off the west coast of Western Australia

    Torres Strait Islander people come from the islands that lie between Queensland's northern tip and Papua New Guinea.

  5. 5. How many convict ships were in the First Fleet?

    • 7
    • 26
    • 11 (correct)

    The First Fleet was the first group of 11 convict ships to arrive from Great Britain.

  6. 6. When did the First Fleet arrive from Great Britain?

    • 1 January 1901
    • 26 January 1788 (correct)
    • 25 April 1915

    The First Fleet arrived on 26 January 1788, which marked the start of European settlement.

  7. 7. Why did the British Government send convicts to the new colony of New South Wales?

    • Convicts were needed to fight in World War I
    • British jails could not hold the large number of prisoners (correct)
    • Gold had been found in New South Wales

    British laws were harsh at the time and the jails were overcrowded, so convicts were transported to New South Wales.

  8. 8. Who was the first Governor of the colony of New South Wales?

    • Captain James Cook
    • Captain Arthur Phillip (correct)
    • Captain Matthew Flinders

    Captain Arthur Phillip was the first Governor of the colony of New South Wales.

  9. 9. Where did the early free settlers come from?

    • Great Britain and Ireland (correct)
    • China and Japan
    • Italy and Greece

    Early free settlers came from Great Britain and Ireland, and this heritage has strongly shaped Australia's history, culture and politics.

  10. 10. In which year did a gold rush begin in New South Wales and Victoria?

    • 1788
    • 1851 (correct)
    • 1901

    A gold rush began in 1851 when gold was discovered in the colonies of New South Wales and Victoria.

  11. 11. During the gold rush, which people became the first large group of migrants from outside Europe?

    • Chinese people (correct)
    • American people
    • Indian people

    Chinese people who arrived during the gold rush were the first large group of migrants who were not from Europe.

  12. 12. What happened to Australia's population in the 10 years after the gold rush began?

    • It more than doubled (correct)
    • It stayed about the same
    • It fell by half

    The gold rush drew people from around the world, and within 10 years the population more than doubled.

  13. 13. What was formed when the separate colonies united in 1901?

    • The Commonwealth of Australia (correct)
    • The colony of New South Wales
    • The Australian and New Zealand Army Corps

    In 1901 the separate colonies joined together in a federation of states called the Commonwealth of Australia.

  14. 14. After which event were Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples included in official estimates of the Australian population?

    • Federation in 1901
    • The 1967 Referendum (correct)
    • The 1851 gold rush

    Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples were left out of official population estimates until after the 1967 Referendum.

  15. 15. Which group came to Australia in large numbers after World War II?

    • Convicts transported from the jails of Great Britain
    • Gold miners who came from China to look for gold
    • People from Europe who had to leave their homelands (correct)

    After World War II a wave of non-British migration arrived, as millions of people in Europe had to leave their homelands.

  16. 16. How many distinct Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages are there?

    • Fewer than 10
    • More than 100 (correct)
    • About 25

    There are more than 100 distinct Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages, and they are valued in Australia.

  17. 17. How many states and mainland territories does Australia have?

    • Six states and two mainland territories (correct)
    • Seven states and one mainland territory
    • Five states and three mainland territories

    Australia is a federation of six states and two mainland territories.

  18. 18. Which city is Australia's national capital?

    • Sydney
    • Melbourne
    • Canberra (correct)

    Canberra is Australia's capital city, and each state and mainland territory also has its own capital.

  19. 19. What is the capital city of Western Australia?

    • Adelaide
    • Darwin
    • Perth (correct)

    Perth is the capital of Western Australia, the largest state.

  20. 20. Brisbane is the capital city of which state?

    • New South Wales
    • Victoria
    • Queensland (correct)

    Brisbane is the capital of Queensland, Australia's second largest state.

  21. 21. Which was the first colony established by the British in Australia?

    • New South Wales (correct)
    • South Australia
    • Western Australia

    New South Wales was the first colony the British established, and its capital, Sydney, is now the nation's largest city.

  22. 22. Which is Australia's largest state?

    • Queensland
    • New South Wales
    • Western Australia (correct)

    Western Australia is the largest state, and Queensland is the second largest.

  23. 23. Which is Australia's smallest state?

    • Tasmania (correct)
    • Victoria
    • South Australia

    Tasmania is the smallest state, while Victoria is the smallest of the mainland states.

  24. 24. What separates Tasmania from the mainland?

    • Torres Strait
    • Bass Strait (correct)
    • The Great Barrier Reef

    Tasmania is an island state, separated from the mainland by Bass Strait.

  25. 25. The Great Barrier Reef runs along the coast of which state?

    • Queensland (correct)
    • Western Australia
    • South Australia

    The world-famous Great Barrier Reef runs along Queensland's eastern coast.

  26. 26. Uluru, Kata Tjuta and Kings Canyon are icons of which part of Australia?

    • South Australia
    • The Northern Territory (correct)
    • Queensland

    Uluru, Kata Tjuta and Kings Canyon are icons of the Northern Territory, whose capital is Darwin.

  27. 27. Cradle Mountain, Port Arthur and the Bay of Fires are icons of which state?

    • Victoria
    • New South Wales
    • Tasmania (correct)

    Cradle Mountain, Port Arthur and the Bay of Fires are all Tasmanian icons.

  28. 28. Where are Parliament House and the High Court of Australia located?

    • Sydney, in the state of New South Wales
    • Darwin, in the Northern Territory
    • Canberra, in the Australian Capital Territory (correct)

    The Australian Capital Territory lies between Sydney and Melbourne and contains Canberra, home to national institutions such as Parliament House and the High Court.

  29. 29. Who performs a Welcome to Country?

    • The most senior politician or official at the event
    • An Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander custodian of the local region (correct)
    • Any guest at the event, whatever their background

    A Welcome to Country is performed by a local Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander custodian to welcome visitors to their traditional land.

  30. 30. Who can deliver an Acknowledgement of Country?

    • Anyone (correct)
    • Only an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander Elder
    • Only the Governor-General

    Anyone can give an Acknowledgement of Country to show respect for the Traditional Custodians of the land.

  31. 31. On which date is Australia Day celebrated each year?

    • 26 January (correct)
    • 25 April
    • 1 January

    Australia Day is celebrated on 26 January and is a public holiday in every state and territory.

  32. 32. What anniversary does Australia Day mark?

    • The arrival of the First Fleet from Great Britain in 1788 (correct)
    • The joining of the colonies into one nation in 1901
    • The landing of Australian troops at Gallipoli in 1915

    Australia Day is the anniversary of the First Fleet's arrival from Great Britain in 1788.

  33. 33. Who announces the Australian of the Year Awards during Australia Day celebrations?

    • The Governor-General
    • The Premier of New South Wales
    • The Prime Minister (correct)

    During Australia Day celebrations, the Prime Minister announces the Australian of the Year Awards in Canberra.

  34. 34. On which date is Anzac Day commemorated each year?

    • 26 January
    • 25 April (correct)
    • 1 January

    Anzac Day is commemorated on 25 April, the anniversary of the 1915 landing at Gallipoli.

  35. 35. Where did the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps land on 25 April 1915?

    • Darwin, in the Northern Territory
    • Gallipoli, in Türkiye (correct)
    • Hobart, in Tasmania

    Anzac Day is named after the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps, which landed at Gallipoli in Türkiye during World War I.

  36. 36. Who do Australians remember on Anzac Day?

    • The convicts and sailors who arrived on the First Fleet in 1788
    • All Australians who served and died in wars, conflicts and peacekeeping operations (correct)
    • The leaders who joined the colonies together to form one nation

    Anzac Day is a solemn day to remember the sacrifice of Australians who served and died in wars, conflicts and peacekeeping operations.

  37. 37. What are the colours of the Australian National Flag?

    • Green and gold
    • Blue, white and red (correct)
    • Black, red and yellow

    The Australian National Flag is blue, white and red.

  38. 38. How many points does the Commonwealth Star on the Australian National Flag have?

    • Five
    • Six
    • Seven (correct)

    The Commonwealth Star has seven points: one for each of the six states and one for the territories.

  39. 39. What does the Union Jack on the Australian National Flag represent?

    • The six states and the territories
    • Australia's history of British settlement (correct)
    • The stars seen in the southern sky

    The Union Jack in the top left corner represents Australia's history of British settlement and the laws and institutions inherited from it.

  40. 40. Which flag is black, red and yellow?

    • The Torres Strait Islander Flag
    • The Australian Aboriginal Flag (correct)
    • The Australian National Flag

    The Australian Aboriginal Flag is black, red and yellow.

  41. 41. On the Australian Aboriginal Flag, what does the yellow circle represent?

    • The sun (correct)
    • The earth
    • The sea

    The yellow circle stands for the sun; the black top half represents Aboriginal peoples and the red bottom half represents the earth.

  42. 42. On the Torres Strait Islander Flag, what does the blue panel in the centre represent?

    • The sky
    • The land
    • The sea (correct)

    The blue panel represents the sea, while the green stripes represent the land.

  43. 43. On the Torres Strait Islander Flag, what is the symbol for all Torres Strait Islanders?

    • The yellow circle
    • The Southern Cross
    • The white dancer's headdress (correct)

    The white dancer's headdress in the centre of the flag is a symbol for all Torres Strait Islanders.

  44. 44. Which animals support the shield on the Commonwealth Coat of Arms?

    • A koala and a platypus
    • A kangaroo and an emu (correct)
    • A kangaroo and a koala

    A kangaroo and an emu, both native Australian animals, hold up the shield, one on each side.

  45. 45. What does the Commonwealth Coat of Arms identify?

    • The authority and property of the Commonwealth of Australia (correct)
    • The members and leaders of the largest political party
    • The capital cities of each of the states and territories

    The Commonwealth Coat of Arms is the official symbol of the Commonwealth and identifies its authority and property.

  46. 46. What does the shield on the Commonwealth Coat of Arms represent?

    • The two mainland territories
    • The ships of the First Fleet
    • The six states and federation (correct)

    The shield in the centre of the Coat of Arms represents the six states and federation.

  47. 47. What is Australia's national flower?

    • The red rose
    • The lotus
    • The golden wattle (correct)

    The golden wattle is Australia's national flower, and it also forms the background of the Coat of Arms.

  48. 48. In which season does the golden wattle have its golden yellow flowers?

    • Spring (correct)
    • Winter
    • Autumn

    The golden wattle is a small tree with bright green leaves and many golden yellow flowers in spring.

  49. 49. What are Australia's national colours?

    • Green and gold (correct)
    • Blue and white
    • Red and black

    Green and gold are the national colours because they are the colours of the golden wattle.

  50. 50. What is Australia's national gemstone?

    • The opal (correct)
    • The diamond
    • The sapphire

    The opal is the national gemstone; an Aboriginal legend says a rainbow touching the earth created its colours.

  51. 51. Which song is Australia's national anthem?

    • Waltzing Matilda
    • Land of the Southern Cross
    • Advance Australia Fair (correct)

    'Advance Australia Fair' is the national anthem, sung at events such as citizenship ceremonies and major sporting events.
