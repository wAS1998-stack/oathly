Part 4: Australian values

50 practice questions with answers and explanations for Part 4 of the Australian citizenship test. The correct answer is marked under each question.

These are the questions you cannot get wrong. The real test includes 5 Australian values questions and you must answer all of them correctly to pass.

Take a practice test

  1. 1. Which ideas are Australian values based on?

    • Wealth, social class and family background
    • Loyalty to one religion and one political party
    • Freedom, respect, fairness and equality of opportunity (correct)

    Australian values are based on freedom, respect, fairness and equality of opportunity, and they help keep Australia secure, prosperous and peaceful.

  2. 2. What are all Australians expected to do with Australian values?

    • Follow them only when it suits them
    • Respect, share and uphold them (correct)
    • Replace them with the values of their home country

    Australian values are shared by everyone, and all Australians are expected to respect, share and uphold them.

  3. 3. When should you follow Australian laws?

    • Only when the police can see you
    • At all times, even when no one is watching (correct)
    • Only when the law agrees with your culture

    Everybody should obey the law at all times, and you should follow it even if no one is watching.

  4. 4. Do Australian laws apply to people of every background and culture?

    • No, migrants can follow the laws of their home country
    • No, only people born in Australia must follow them
    • Yes, everyone in Australia must follow Australian laws (correct)

    Whatever your background or culture, you must follow Australian laws, and no person or group is above the law.

  5. 5. What happens to people who break the law in Australia?

    • They face penalties (correct)
    • Nothing, if they did not know about the law
    • Nothing, if they are community leaders

    Everybody should obey the law and not break it at any time, otherwise they will face penalties.

  6. 6. In Australia, who determines the laws?

    • Religious leaders
    • Wealthy business owners
    • Parliaments elected by the people (correct)

    In Australia's parliamentary democracy, laws are determined by parliaments elected by the people, so citizens are involved in how the country is governed.

  7. 7. Under freedom of speech, how freely can people in Australia express their ideas?

    • Without any limits, even if it breaks the law
    • Only with permission from the government
    • Freely, as long as it is within the law (correct)

    People in Australia can express their ideas freely and say or write what they think, but they must stay within the law.

  8. 8. Which statement about newspapers, television and radio in Australia is correct?

    • They must get government approval for every story
    • They are not allowed to report on protests
    • They have the same freedom of speech as people (correct)

    Newspapers, television and radio outlets have the same freedom of speech that people in Australia have.

  9. 9. Is it acceptable to promote violence against a group because of their religion or culture?

    • No, it is against Australian values and law (correct)
    • Yes, it is part of freedom of speech
    • Yes, if it is only done online

    It is never acceptable to promote violence against a person or group, for example because of their culture, ethnicity, religion or background.

  10. 10. Which of these is illegal in Australia?

    • Criticising a government decision
    • Writing to your member of parliament
    • Making false allegations (correct)

    Freedom of speech has limits: it is illegal to make false allegations or to encourage others to break the law.

  11. 11. Why is peaceful public protest tolerated in Australia?

    • It gives protesters the right to damage property
    • It replaces the need for elections
    • It is an essential part of democracy (correct)

    People may protest peacefully against the government, because tolerating peaceful public protest is an essential part of democracy.

  12. 12. Can a person be forced to leave a lawful group they belong to?

    • Yes, if their family wants them to leave
    • Yes, if their employer wants them to leave
    • No, people are free to join or leave groups voluntarily (correct)

    People are free to join or leave any group voluntarily, as long as it is within the law, and no one can be forced to join an organisation or to leave it.

  13. 13. What legal status do religious laws have in Australia?

    • The same status as Australian law
    • They replace Australian law for members of that religion
    • They have no legal status (correct)

    Religious laws have no legal status in Australia, and Australian law applies to everyone.

  14. 14. Which statement about polygamy (being married to more than one person at the same time) is correct?

    • It is legal if a person's religion allows it
    • It is against the law in Australia (correct)
    • It is legal if all the partners agree

    Polygamy is against the law in Australia and can lead to severe legal penalties, including imprisonment.

  15. 15. What is the legal position of forced marriage in Australia?

    • It is against the law and can lead to imprisonment (correct)
    • It is allowed if both families agree
    • It is allowed if it is a cultural tradition

    Forced marriage, where one or both people did not freely choose to marry, is illegal and can result in severe penalties, including imprisonment.

  16. 16. How is religious intolerance viewed in Australian society?

    • It is acceptable towards small religions
    • It is not acceptable (correct)
    • It is acceptable if your own religion allows it

    Religious intolerance is not acceptable, and everyone should have an equal opportunity to pursue their goals regardless of their religion.

  17. 17. Under Australian law, how are Buddhists and people of other faiths treated compared with Christians?

    • They receive the same treatment (correct)
    • They receive less legal protection
    • They must follow Christian rules

    People from different backgrounds are not given preferential treatment; Buddhists and people of all other faiths are treated the same as Christians.

  18. 18. Under Australian law, who can marry each other?

    • Only a man and a woman
    • Two people, including two men or two women (correct)
    • One person and more than one partner at the same time

    Under Australian law two people can marry each other, including two men or two women, but polygamy is illegal.

  19. 19. Can women in Australia stand for parliament and join the police?

    • No, only men can do these things
    • Only with their husband's permission
    • Yes, women and men have equal rights (correct)

    Men and women have equal rights, including equal access to education and work, voting, standing for parliament, joining the Defence Force and police, and equal treatment in court.

  20. 20. In Australia, can a woman get a job ahead of a man?

    • No, a man must always be chosen first
    • Yes, if she has better qualifications and skills (correct)
    • Only if no men apply for the job

    Gender discrimination is against the law, and a woman has the right to get a job ahead of a man if she has better qualifications and skills.

  21. 21. Who makes decisions about a person's personal matters, such as marriage and religion?

    • The person's parents
    • The person themselves (correct)
    • The person's religious leader

    Everyone can make their own independent choices about personal matters such as marriage and religion, and the law protects them from intimidation or violence.

  22. 22. Which statement about physical violence against a spouse or partner is correct?

    • It is never acceptable and is a criminal offence (correct)
    • It is acceptable if it happens at home
    • It is a private family matter, not a crime

    Physical violence against a spouse or partner is never acceptable and is a criminal offence in Australia.

  23. 23. Who can apply to the courts for a divorce in Australia?

    • Only the husband
    • Only a couple who both want the divorce
    • Either a husband or a wife (correct)

    Divorce is acceptable in Australia, and either spouse may apply to the courts even if the other wants to stay married.

  24. 24. In Australia, what should a person get a job based on?

    • Their gender
    • Their skills and experience (correct)
    • Their family's wealth

    Under a 'fair go', people should get jobs because of their skills and experience, not their gender, wealth or ethnicity.

  25. 25. What should a person's achievements in life be the result of?

    • Their social class
    • Their hard work and talents (correct)
    • Their ethnic background

    Australians believe everyone deserves a 'fair go', so what someone achieves should come from their hard work and talents.

  26. 26. How have many new migrants become leaders in business, the arts, public service and sport?

    • Through family connections
    • Through wealth they brought with them
    • Through hard work and enterprise (correct)

    Many new migrants have become leaders in Australia through their hard work and enterprise.

  27. 27. Which statement about verbal and physical abuse is correct?

    • Both are illegal (correct)
    • Only physical abuse is illegal
    • Verbal abuse is legal if it happens at home

    Violence of any kind, including verbal and physical abuse, is illegal in Australia.

  28. 28. How do Australians believe disagreements should be handled?

    • Peacefully, with everyone free from violence and intimidation (correct)
    • With threats, if the issue is serious enough
    • With violence, if talking does not work

    Australians believe in peaceful disagreement and, above all, in everyone's right to be safe and free from violence and intimidation.

  29. 29. What is the age of sexual consent in Australia?

    • 14 in every state and territory
    • 16 or 17, depending on the state or territory (correct)
    • 21 in every state and territory

    The age of sexual consent is 16 or 17, depending on which state or territory you live in.

  30. 30. A 20-year-old man wants to have sexual relations with a 15-year-old girl. Which statement is correct?

    • It is legal if her parents agree
    • It is illegal in every state and territory (correct)
    • It is legal in some states and territories

    Because the age of consent is 16 or 17, this would be illegal everywhere in Australia.

  31. 31. What should you do when the police make a lawful request?

    • Obey it (correct)
    • Ignore it if you disagree
    • Obey it only if a lawyer is present

    You should obey a lawful request from the police, because all Australians commit to following the law.

  32. 32. What does mutual respect mean?

    • Listening to others and respecting their views, even when they differ from yours (correct)
    • Agreeing with everyone in your community to avoid any argument
    • Respecting only the people who share your own background and beliefs

    Mutual respect means listening to others and respecting their views and opinions, even when they are different from your own.

  33. 33. Racism has no place in Australia. Which of these is an example of racism?

    • Making racially abusive comments at a sporting event (correct)
    • Cheering for a team from another country
    • Learning about another person's culture

    Racism has no place in Australia; examples include racially abusive comments in public or at sporting events, and sharing racially offensive material online.

  34. 34. What is everyone in Australia expected to try to do when they are able to?

    • Rely on others to support them
    • Support themselves and their family (correct)
    • Leave all community work to the government

    Everyone is expected to contribute to the community and to try to support themselves and their family when they can.

  35. 35. What does 'mateship' mean in Australia?

    • Spending time only with people from your own country
    • Helping each other in times of need (correct)
    • Supporting only the members of your own family

    Australians value mateship, which means helping others, and accepting help, especially in difficult times.

  36. 36. Which of these is an example of compassion for those in need?

    • Only helping people who can pay you back
    • Staying away from neighbours who are lonely
    • Driving a friend to a medical appointment (correct)

    Showing compassion might mean taking a meal to an elderly neighbour, driving a friend to a medical appointment, or visiting someone who is sick, frail or lonely.

  37. 37. How strong is Australia's tradition of community service and volunteering?

    • Strong, with many opportunities to volunteer (correct)
    • Weak, because volunteering is not encouraged
    • Non-existent, because all helpers must be paid

    Australia has a strong tradition of volunteering, which is a great way to share knowledge and learn new skills.

  38. 38. What role does English play in Australian society?

    • It is the national language and an important unifying element (correct)
    • It is one of several official national languages
    • It is only needed by people who work for the government

    English is Australia's national language and an important unifying element of society, so people living here should make an effort to learn it.

  39. 39. If a citizenship applicant's native language is a language other than English, what must they do?

    • Make reasonable efforts to learn English (correct)
    • Stop speaking their first language
    • Pay someone to speak English for them

    Applicants for citizenship must make reasonable efforts to learn English if it is not their native language.

  40. 40. How does learning English help people living in Australia?

    • It lets them avoid obeying the law
    • It helps them get an education and a job, and integrate (correct)
    • It lets them vote without enrolling

    English helps people get an education and a job and better integrate into the community.

  41. 41. What should you do if you suspect a friend or neighbour may be planning a serious crime?

    • Report it to Australian government authorities as soon as possible (correct)
    • Keep it secret to protect your friend or neighbour
    • Wait until the crime happens and then tell someone

    Everyone has a responsibility to help protect Australian society, which includes reporting a suspected serious crime as soon as possible.

  42. 42. If you know that a child is being abused, what should you do?

    • Keep it within the family
    • Report it to the police (correct)
    • Wait until the child asks for help

    Anyone in Australia who sees or knows of a child being abused should report it to the police to investigate.

  43. 43. Which of these is an example of cyber abuse?

    • Sending a birthday message to a friend on social media
    • Joining an online study group for the citizenship test
    • Sharing sexual photos of someone online without their consent (correct)

    Online abuse is not accepted in Australia, and many forms of cyber abuse, such as sharing sexual images without consent or stalking someone online, are illegal.

  44. 44. In the citizenship pledge, who do Australian citizens pledge their loyalty to?

    • Australia and its people (correct)
    • The political party they vote for
    • The country where they were born

    In the citizenship pledge, new citizens pledge their loyalty to Australia and its people.

  45. 45. Can an Australian citizen also hold the citizenship of another country?

    • No, never
    • Yes, but only if they were born in Australia
    • Yes, if the laws of that country allow it (correct)

    Australian citizens may also hold the citizenship of another country if that country's laws allow it; this is called dual or multiple citizenship.

  46. 46. An Australian citizen also holds the citizenship of another country. Which laws must they follow while in Australia?

    • The laws of their other country
    • All Australian laws, at all times (correct)
    • Only the Australian laws they agree with

    An Australian citizen in Australia must follow all Australian laws at all times, even if they hold another citizenship.

  47. 47. Which law must Australian citizens follow even when they are overseas?

    • The ban on sexual activity with a child under 16 (correct)
    • The local council rules on parking and noise
    • The state rules on public transport tickets

    Some Australian laws apply to citizens overseas; for example, it is illegal to be involved in any sexual activity with a child under 16, in Australia or overseas.

  48. 48. Which of these would undermine Australia's interests and security?

    • Voting for any candidate in a federal election
    • Using social media to share official government secrets (correct)
    • Volunteering for a local charity or sports club

    Australians share an obligation not to undermine Australia's interests and security, for example by sharing official government secrets on social media.

  49. 49. What would promoting distrust in and fear of an ethnic community do?

    • Damage Australian community relations (correct)
    • Make the community safer
    • Strengthen national unity

    Promoting distrust in and fear of an ethnic community would damage community relations in Australia.

  50. 50. Under freedom of speech, where can people meet for social or political discussion?

    • Only in government buildings
    • Only in places of worship
    • In public or private places (correct)

    Freedom of speech means people can meet in public or private to discuss social or political matters, as long as they stay within the law.
