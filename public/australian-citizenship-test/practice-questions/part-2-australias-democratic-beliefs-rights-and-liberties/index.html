Part 2: Australia's democratic beliefs, rights and liberties

42 practice questions with answers and explanations for Part 2 of the Australian citizenship test. The correct answer is marked under each question.

Take a practice test

  1. 1. What is Australia's system of government?

    • A one-party state
    • A parliamentary democracy (correct)
    • A military government

    Australia's system of government is a parliamentary democracy, where the people vote for representatives in parliament.

  2. 2. In Australia's parliamentary democracy, where does the power of government come from?

    • Religious leaders
    • The police
    • The Australian people (correct)

    The power of government comes from the Australian people, who vote for their representatives in parliament.

  3. 3. How do representatives in parliament answer to the people for their decisions?

    • Through the courts
    • Through the police
    • Through elections (correct)

    Representatives in parliament must answer to the people, through elections, for the decisions they make.

  4. 4. What does the Rule of Law mean?

    • All Australians are equal under the law (correct)
    • Laws apply only to Australian citizens
    • Leaders can decide which laws to follow

    Under the Rule of Law, all Australians are equal under the law and no one is above it.

  5. 5. Who must obey Australia's laws?

    • Everyone, including leaders and the police (correct)
    • Everyone except government leaders
    • Only people born in Australia

    No person, group or religious rule is above the law, and this includes government, community and religious leaders, business people and the police.

  6. 6. According to Australia's democratic beliefs, how should change happen?

    • Through discussion, peaceful persuasion and the democratic process (correct)
    • Through violence if other ways are too slow
    • Through the decisions of religious leaders

    Australians believe change should come through discussion, peaceful persuasion and the democratic process, and they reject violence.

  7. 7. How are Australians expected to treat each other, whatever their background?

    • According to their wealth
    • According to their religion
    • With dignity and respect (correct)

    All Australians are expected to treat each other with dignity and respect, regardless of things like race, gender, religion or wealth.

  8. 8. Which freedom allows people to say and write what they think?

    • Freedom of association
    • Freedom of speech (correct)
    • Freedom of religion

    Freedom of speech means people can say and write what they think and discuss their ideas with others.

  9. 9. As long as they obey the law, what can people in Australia do under freedom of speech?

    • Damage property to protest against the government
    • Threaten people who disagree with them
    • Criticise the government and protest peacefully (correct)

    People can criticise the government, protest peacefully and campaign to change laws, as long as they obey Australian laws.

  10. 10. Which freedom covers expressing views through art, film, music and literature?

    • Freedom of association
    • Freedom of expression (correct)
    • Freedom of religion

    Freedom of expression means people can express their views, including through art, film, music and literature.

  11. 11. What is freedom of association?

    • The right to follow any religion you choose
    • The right to form and join groups to pursue common goals (correct)
    • The right to vote in a secret ballot

    Freedom of association is the right to form and join associations, such as a political party or trade union, to pursue common goals.

  12. 12. Under freedom of association, can a person be forced to join a group?

    • No, people can choose not to join (correct)
    • Yes, everyone must join a trade union
    • Yes, voters must join a political party

    People may join any legal organisation, but they can also decide not to join and cannot be forced to.

  13. 13. When people gather to protest, what must the gathering be like?

    • Peaceful, with no one hurt and no property damaged (correct)
    • Approved in advance by a religious or community leader
    • Organised and led by a political party

    Australians can gather to protest, but the gathering must be peaceful, must not injure anyone or damage property, and must obey the law.

  14. 14. What religious heritage does Australia have?

    • A Judaeo-Christian heritage (correct)
    • A Buddhist heritage
    • A Hindu heritage

    Australia has a Judaeo-Christian heritage and many Australians describe themselves as Christians, while people from all the large religions live here.

  15. 15. Which of these is a public holiday because it is a Christian day?

    • Good Friday (correct)
    • Anzac Day
    • Australia Day

    Australia has public holidays on Christian days such as Good Friday, Easter Sunday and Christmas Day.

  16. 16. What does it mean that the government in Australia is secular?

    • It follows the rules of one official religion
    • It is run by the leaders of the largest church
    • It operates separately from churches and other religious groups (correct)

    A secular government operates separately from churches or other religious entities and treats all citizens equally, whatever their beliefs.

  17. 17. Does Australia have an official national religion?

    • Yes, the official religion is Christianity
    • No, people can follow any religion or none (correct)
    • Yes, each state chooses its own religion

    Australia has no official national religion, and people are free to follow any religion they choose, or none.

  18. 18. If an Australian law and a religious practice conflict, which one prevails?

    • The religious practice
    • The Australian law (correct)
    • Whichever the religious leader chooses

    Religious practices must obey Australian law, and where the two conflict, Australian law prevails.

  19. 19. What rights do men and women have in Australia?

    • Men have more rights than women
    • Rights depend on whether a person is married
    • Equal rights (correct)

    Men and women have equal rights in Australia, and it is against the law to discriminate against someone because of their gender.

  20. 20. What does Australia's value of a 'fair go' aim to ensure?

    • That wealthy families get the best jobs
    • That there are no formal class distinctions (correct)
    • That older people always come first

    A 'fair go' means equal opportunity, where success comes from talent, work and effort rather than wealth or background.

  21. 21. Which of these is a responsibility of Australian citizenship?

    • Joining a political party
    • Serving on a jury if called to do so (correct)
    • Joining a trade union

    The responsibilities of citizenship are to obey the law, vote, defend Australia should the need arise, and serve on a jury if called.

  22. 22. Which of these is a privilege of Australian citizenship?

    • Obeying the laws of Australia
    • Serving on a jury if called
    • Applying for an Australian passport (correct)

    Applying for an Australian passport and re-entering Australia freely is a privilege, while obeying the law and jury service are responsibilities.

  23. 23. Which of these is listed as both a responsibility and a privilege of Australian citizenship?

    • Voting in elections and referendums (correct)
    • Serving on a jury if called
    • Applying for a passport

    Voting in federal and state or territory elections, and in a referendum, appears in both lists.

  24. 24. At what age do Australian citizens have a responsibility to vote?

    • 18 years or over (correct)
    • 16 years or over
    • 21 years or over

    Voting is a responsibility, right and privilege for Australian citizens aged 18 years or over.

  25. 25. What is a referendum?

    • A vote to choose the Prime Minister
    • A vote to change the Australian Constitution (correct)
    • A vote to elect local councillors

    A referendum is a vote on changing the Australian Constitution, and citizens aged 18 or over have a responsibility to vote in it.

  26. 26. In which elections is voting compulsory?

    • Only local government elections
    • Only federal elections
    • Federal and state or territory elections (correct)

    Voting is compulsory in federal and state or territory elections.

  27. 27. In some states, voting may be optional in which elections?

    • Federal elections
    • Local government elections (correct)
    • State elections

    In some states it may not be compulsory to vote in local government elections, unlike federal and state or territory elections.

  28. 28. Is service in the Australian Defence Force compulsory?

    • Yes, for all citizens aged 18
    • No, it is voluntary (correct)
    • Yes, for all men aged 18 to 25

    Service in the Australian Defence Force is voluntary, but citizens have a responsibility to defend Australia should the need arise.

  29. 29. What does a jury do?

    • Makes new laws for the parliament
    • Hears the evidence and decides if a person is guilty (correct)
    • Chooses which judge hears a case

    A jury is a group of ordinary Australians who listen to the evidence in a court case and decide whether a person is guilty or not guilty.

  30. 30. Which Australian citizens can be called to serve on a jury?

    • Only citizens who are lawyers
    • Citizens who are on the electoral roll (correct)
    • Only citizens who work for the government

    Citizens on the electoral roll can be called for jury service, which is a responsibility for citizens aged 18 or over.

  31. 31. What does jury service help to ensure?

    • That parliament can pass laws faster
    • That the court system is open and fair (correct)
    • That the police decide who is guilty

    When ordinary citizens serve on juries, it helps keep the court system open and fair.

  32. 32. Which of these is a job in the Australian Public Service?

    • Working at the Australian Taxation Office (correct)
    • Working at a private bank
    • Running your own small business

    Citizens can apply to work for the Australian Government in the Australian Public Service, for example at Services Australia or the ATO.

  33. 33. What does the Australian Defence Force include?

    • The police and ambulance services
    • The Australian Federal Police
    • The Army, Navy and Air Force (correct)

    The Australian Defence Force is made up of the Army, Navy and Air Force, and citizens can apply to work in it.

  34. 34. Which Australian citizens can seek election to parliament?

    • Those aged 18 or over who are not dual citizens (correct)
    • Any citizen aged 16 or over
    • Only citizens who were born in Australia

    Citizens aged 18 or over who do not hold dual citizenship can stand for election to federal, state or territory parliament.

  35. 35. When an Australian citizen returns from overseas, do they need a visa to re-enter Australia?

    • No, citizens can return without a visa (correct)
    • Yes, they need a tourist visa
    • Yes, unless they travelled for work

    Australian citizens have the right to live freely in Australia and can return from overseas without needing a visa.

  36. 36. While overseas, whose laws must Australian citizens obey?

    • Only Australian laws
    • Only the laws they agree with
    • The laws of the country they are in (correct)

    Australian citizens overseas can ask Australian officials for help, but they must obey the laws of the country they are in.

  37. 37. How can Australian officials help citizens who are overseas in an emergency?

    • By paying any fines they receive
    • By excusing them from local laws
    • By issuing an emergency passport (correct)

    Australian officials at embassies, high commissions and consulates can help in emergencies, for example by issuing an emergency passport.

  38. 38. If an Australian citizen has a child born overseas, what can they apply for?

    • For the child to become a citizen by descent (correct)
    • For the child to be exempt from Australian law
    • For the child to vote before turning 18

    One privilege of citizenship is applying for a child born overseas to become an Australian citizen by descent.

  39. 39. Who collects tax in Australia?

    • Services Australia
    • The Australian Electoral Commission
    • The Australian Taxation Office (ATO) (correct)

    Paying tax is required by law, and tax on money earned from a job, business or investments is collected by the ATO.

  40. 40. What are taxes spent on?

    • Campaigns run by political parties
    • Private homes for new citizens
    • Services such as healthcare, education and defence (correct)

    Taxes pay for services including government-funded healthcare and education, defence, roads and railways, and social security.

  41. 41. Why is freedom of speech important in Australia?

    • It allows people to make false claims about others
    • It lets people ignore laws they dislike
    • It underpins Australia's democratic system (correct)

    Freedom of speech is a core Australian value that underpins the democratic system, but Australian laws must still be obeyed.

  42. 42. Why do our representatives in government make laws?

    • To keep society orderly, free and safe and protect rights (correct)
    • To protect the interests of wealthy people
    • To give political parties more power

    Representatives make laws to maintain an orderly, free and safe society and to protect people's rights, and everyone in Australia must obey them.
